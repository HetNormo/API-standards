### EDA-P-05: Event type is driven by context and business intent

#### Description

As defined in **EDA-P-04**, every event is either an event notification or an event-carried state transfer. This principle **EDA-P-05** governs how that choice is made.

These two event types represent different trade-offs in autonomy, coupling, data exposure, and operational behavior. The choice between them must be made explicitly and deliberately for each event, based on business intent and contextual constraints rather than technical defaults or implicit preferences.
To ensure consistent, transparent, and well-founded decisions across the energy sector, this choice must be guided by the **State Transfer versus Notification decision tree** (Appendix A). The decision tree provides a structured evaluation of legal, architectural, operational, and governance criteria and makes trade-offs explicit.

#### Rationale

*	<u>Intentional architecture</u>: Without an explicit decision, event types are often chosen implicitly, leading to unintended runtime dependencies, inconsistent consumer expectations, or unnecessary data exposure. Making the choice deliberate prevents accidental coupling and clarifies the semantics of each event.
*	<u>Consistency and cross-domain interoperability</u>: A shared decision framework promotes consistent event design across domains and organizations.
*	<u>Governance and future adaptability</u>: Explicitly documenting why a particular event type was chosen supports architectural governance and safe evolution. When business requirements, regulatory conditions, or consumer landscapes change, teams can re-evaluate decisions using the same decision tree instead of redesigning events ad hoc.

#### Implication

*	<u>Explicit architectural decision required</u>: For every business event, teams must explicitly decide whether it is modeled as an event notification or an event-carried state transfer.
*	<u>Decision tree as mandatory guidance</u>: The State Transfer versus Notification decision tree (Appendix A) must be used to guide this decision, ensuring that legal, privacy, architectural, and operational considerations are systematically evaluated.
*	<u>Documented rationale</u>: The outcome of the decision - including the dominant drivers (for example privacy constraints, consumer autonomy, or payload considerations) - should be documented as part of the event design, such as in AsyncAPI documentation or an architectural decision record.
*	<u>Re-evaluation over time</u>: As business intent, regulatory requirements, or consumer needs evolve, previously made decisions may require reassessment. The same decision tree should be used to evaluate whether the chosen event type remains appropriate.

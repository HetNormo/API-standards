### EDA-P-02: Events are business events

| Strategy | Events are business events. |

#### Description

A business event reflects a meaningful change in the business, like 'meter connected' or 'customer account opened', while a technical event relates to internal system actions, such as 'cache refreshed' or 'database row updated'. Business events clearly describe what happens in the business domain and are easy for everyone to understand, whereas technical events focus on implementation details that do not convey business value. In most cases, consumers want to receive notifications about (relevant) business events [Project Notifications 2022].

DDD distinguishes between domain events and system events: a domain event captures something that occurred in the business - expressed in the domain's ubiquitous language and conveying business meaning - whereas a system event records internal software or infrastructure activity (for example, logs, state transitions, or replication) and serves technical or operational purposes [Evans 2015].

Events represent state transitions of business objects. This principle ensures that events maintain a clear relationship to the domain model's entities and their lifecycle stages. Business events are the only events shared between domains. Business events should be reusable, discoverable and subscribable.

Technical events examples:
   + ButtonClicked
   + S3ObjectCreated
   + CacheInvalidated

   These events are useful for orchestrating technical workflows or debugging, but usually don't resonate with non-technical stakeholders.

Business events examples:
   + CustomerCreated
   + WorkActivityPlanned
   + MeterReadingUpdated
   
These events are easily understood by both technical teams and business stakeholders and directly support domain processes.

#### Rationale

*	<u>Clarity of intent</u>: This principle makes the semantics of the event more explicit. Modeling events as business facts ties them to the domain's ubiquitous language, making event payloads and names meaningful to domain experts, developers, and stakeholders. This reduces ambiguity when interpreting event meaning and leads to more reliable integration contracts.
*	<u>Clear ownership and responsibility</u>: If events carry business meaning, domain teams have clear ownership (schema, semantics, lifecycle). Technical/system events are owned by platform/infra teams - this distinction clarifies change processes and access control.
*	<u>Security & privacy alignment</u>: When events are designed around business needs, it's clearer which personal or sensitive fields are required and which can be omitted, redacted, or pseudonymized - reducing unnecessary PII propagation compared to emitting raw technical traces.
*	<u>Replayability and rebuilding state</u>: If events record domain facts, you can reliably replay them to rebuild projections, recreate system state for a point in time, or recover from failures. System/technical events typically lack the semantic completeness needed for correct state reconstruction.
*	<u>Auditability and traceability</u>: Business events naturally serve as an auditable trail of domain activity (who did what and when). When events are first-class business facts, they support forensic analysis, regulatory compliance, and dispute resolution more easily than low-level system logs.

#### Implication

*   <u>Naming conventions</u>: as events are primarily business events they represent a state of change of business objects, naming these events should be based on the corresponding business objects in Nbility. Technical events should not follow the Nbility framework’s naming. 
*	<u>Routing & policies</u>: Separate topics/streams for technical vs business events; different retention and access controls.
*	<u>Consumer identification</u>: Stating that events are primarily business events determines and identifies categorization of consumer(s)
*	<u>Ownership</u>: Domain teams approve business event schemas; platform teams approve system/technical schemas.
*	<u>Discoverability & subcriberability</u>: client developers may subscribe to an event in the same way, and from the same place as they would subscribe to an API - via your API client/developer portal.
